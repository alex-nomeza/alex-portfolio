## Overview

This selection explores architecture as both spatial design and visual
communication. Drawings, massing models and rendered studies are used together
to test proportion, circulation, material and the behavior of light.

## Process

The work begins with simple volumes and clear relationships between solid and
void. Early studies stay deliberately reduced so that scale, access and spatial
sequence can be evaluated before surface detail is introduced.

Two-dimensional drawings provide control and measurement; three-dimensional
models reveal depth, shadow and viewpoints. Moving repeatedly between both makes
it possible to identify problems early and communicate the intention of a space
more clearly.

## Visual direction

Materials are treated as part of the architectural idea. Pale mineral surfaces,
dark structural lines and restrained terracotta elements create contrast without
overloading the form. Hard directional light is used to expose depth and make
the geometry legible.

## Contribution

The studies cover spatial composition, 2D drawing, 3D modeling, material
exploration and final visualization. The resulting images are not decoration;
they are tools for testing, explaining and refining architectural decisions.
